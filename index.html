Schematic & Connections

Complete Circuit Diagram

Full dual-stage electrical wiring showing the high-speed rotor subsystem (NodeMCU, dual WS2812 strips, A3144 Hall sensor, 10k divider) and independent motor power circuit.

Click the diagram above to inspect full resolution view.

Motor Subsystem (Stator)

Powered by a 4.2V Li-ion battery routed through an ON/OFF switch, TP4056 charging module, and 1N4007 protection diode to drive the 3.5V high-RPM DC motor. A stationary magnet triggers the sensor on each pass.

Rotor Subsystem (Armature)

Powered by an independent 4.2V Li-ion battery with its own TP4056 charger and switch. Steps through a 1N4007 diode down to ~3.5V supplying the NodeMCU ESP8266 and WS2812 strips directly.

A3144 Hall Sensor

Connected to +Vcc, GND, and GPIO13 (D7). Sends exactly 1 ground pulse per revolution to the ESP8266 external interrupt for microsecond-precise zero-degree alignment and RPM calculation.

10kΩ Voltage Divider

Two 10kΩ resistors form a precise 1:1 voltage divider connected to pin A0 (ADC0), stepping battery voltage safely into the 0–1.0V/3.3V ADC range for real-time percentage monitoring.

4. RPM, LED Synchronization & Timing Parameters

Parameter Value / Calculation Formula Technical Purpose
Good Display RPM >= 900 RPM Stable, flicker-free persistence of vision
Revolution Time (T_rev) T_rev = 60 / RPM Total microsecond duration per complete rotation
Nominal Example RPM 1200 RPM Optimal benchmark operating speed
T_rev at 1200 RPM T_rev = 60 / 1200 = 0.05 s = 50 ms Total duration for 360° revolution
Canvas Columns 100 columns Cylindrical display divided into 100 angular slices
Angle per Column Angle = 360° / 100 = 3.6° Precise angular slice allocation per LED update
LED Column Time T_column = 50 ms / 100 = 0.5 ms (500 μs) Microsecond processing budget per column at 1200 RPM
LED Latch Time 60 μs to 80 μs WS2812 protocol reset pulse to latch RGB data
Hall Pulse Glitch Filter 2 ms Rejects false magnetic bounce triggers and electrical noise
